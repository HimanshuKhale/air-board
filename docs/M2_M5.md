The central `InteractionController` consumes complete MediaPipe results, chooses the configured dominant hand, classifies geometric poses, stabilizes entry, maps camera coordinates, and emits the existing typed board commands. Priority is deterministic:

1. global two-hand pause/resume
2. active fist drag
3. active lasso
4. open-palm erase
5. pinch or Stylus Assist write
6. hover

Open palm requires all four non-thumb fingers to pass straightness and wrist-distance tests, then the configured 150â€“250 ms hold. Index-only requires the index extended and middle/ring/pinky folded. Fist requires all four folded. Pinch remains the existing aspect-correct normalized distance with hysteresis and debounce, and takes precedence in the instantaneous pose classifier.

Plane calibration solves an eight-unknown projective homography from four ordered camera points to `(0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1)`. Partial-pivot Gaussian elimination and explicit quadrilateral validation reject unstable geometry. The mapping is applied to every hand-controlled board coordinate before interaction logic.

Lasso selection accepts closed paths with at least 12 points, 180 logical pixels of path, 1800 square pixels of area, and closure inside the configured radius. A non-eraser stroke must have intersecting bounds and at least 30% of its 12-pixel-resampled points inside the polygon.

Fist grab uses the five-landmark palm center. Existing selections can be grabbed from their bounds or near a selected strand. Without selection, the closest non-eraser strand inside the configurable point-to-segment radius is selected. Live drag uses a rendering preview; release stores one `{kind:'move', ids, dx, dy}` action.

The two-hand toggle uses pixel-aspect-correct distance between palm centers, normalized by the smaller camera dimension. Defaults are proximity 0.22, hold 500 ms, cooldown 1300 ms, and separation before rearming.

Stylus Assist uses no pen detector. Its virtual nib is the thumb/index midpoint extended away from the palm center along local hand orientation by 18% of mean palm scale. Pinch still controls pen-down. Calibration saves a normalized board-space x/y offset from the estimated nib to the center target.

All in-flight board interactions reset on tracking loss, dominant-hand/mode change, ownership loss, visibility loss and hand-control changes. Pausing affects hand input only; mouse and touch continue through `InputRouter`.
